    Mack's model: the stochastic layer (first theorem) #

    Mack (1993) works with a random run-off triangle C i k : Ω → ℝ and the σ-algebras D k generated by the first k+1 development years of every accident year. His first assumption, stated conditionally on the row's own history and combined with independence across accident years, gives

    (M1) E[C_{i,k+1} | D_k] = f_k C_{i,k} a.s., for every i and k.

    This file states (M1) in exactly that D_k-conditioned form and proves the first half of Mack's Theorem 2: the chain-ladder factor f̂_k is conditionally unbiased, E[f̂_k | D_k] = f_k, on the event S_k ≠ 0. The argument is Mack's: f̂_k = S_k⁻¹ ∑_i C_{i,k+1}, the factor S_k⁻¹ is D_k-measurable and comes out of the conditional expectation, and (M1) turns the remaining sum into f_k S_k.

    Independence across accident years (M2) is not used here; it is what justifies passing from Mack's row-conditioned assumption to the D_k-conditioned form, and that passage is Independence.lean.

    Type u_1

    A random run-off triangle with n accident and development years, together with the filtration D k of "everything observed up to development year k".

    • C : ℕ → ℕ → Ω → ℝ

      cumulative claims of accident year i at development year k

    • D : ℕ → MeasurableSpace Ω

      the σ-algebra generated by development years 0, …, k (all accident years)

    • D_le (k : ℕ) : self.D k ≤ inst✝
    • D_mono : Monotone self.D

      the D k form a filtration

    • meas (i j k : ℕ) : j ≤ k → MeasureTheory.StronglyMeasurable (self.C i j)

      C i j is D k-measurable as soon as j ≤ k
