    The stochastic over-dispersed Poisson layer #

    Renshaw and Verrall, A stochastic model underlying the chain-ladder technique, British Actuarial Journal 4 (1998) 903-923, introduced the model. England and Verrall (1999), equations (2.5)-(2.7), restate its independent incremental-claims assumptions as

    E[X_{i,k}] = m_{i,k}, Var(X_{i,k}) = phi * m_{i,k}, and log m_{i,k} = c + alpha_i + beta_k, with positive dispersion and the corner constraints alpha_0 = beta_0 = 0.

    England and Verrall, Analytic and bootstrap estimates of prediction errors in claims reserving, Insurance: Mathematics and Economics 25 (1999) 281-293, equations (3.1)-(3.5), and England and Verrall, Stochastic claims reserving in general insurance, British Actuarial Journal 8 (2002) 443-518, equations (7.1)-(7.9), split prediction error into process and estimation components. The single-cell GLM formula uses a delta-method approximation for the estimation variance. This file therefore proves the exact probability identity under explicit independence and unbiasedness hypotheses, while recording the delta-method formula as glmCellMsep and glmAggregateMsep definitions.

    The residual bootstrap follows England and Verrall (1999), equations (4.2)-(4.4), and England and Verrall (2002), equations (8.1) and the following display. englandVerrallBootstrapMean and englandVerrallPredictiveReserve are definitions of the two stages. They make no theorem claiming coverage, consistency or statistical validity. In particular, the process simulator is an input because the first two ODP moments do not select a unique predictive distribution.
