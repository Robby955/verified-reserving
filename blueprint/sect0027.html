27 The stochastic Bornhuetter-Ferguson model

Mack, The Prediction Error of Bornhuetter/Ferguson [ 9 ] , states the increment model on page 91 and derives the prediction-error calculation on pages 95–99. The exact probability statements and the practical assessments are separated below.

Definition 27.1 BF1–BF3 and the future reserve

For zero-based increments \(S_{ik}\), BF1 says that the full family is independent. On a finite horizon \(m\), BF2 and BF3 say

\[ E[S_{ik}]=x_i y_k,\qquad \operatorname {Var}(S_{ik})=x_i\sigma _k^2, \qquad \sum _{k{\lt}m}y_k=1. \]

The true reserve after the first \(d\) periods is \(R_i=\sum _{d\leq k{\lt}m}S_{ik}\), and \(z_d=\sum _{k{\lt}d}y_k\) is the expected reported proportion.

Under BF2 and integrability,

\[ E[R_i]=x_i\sum _{d\leq k{\lt}m}y_k. \]

If also \(d\leq m\), this equals \(x_i(1-z_d)\). Under BF1, BF3, and square integrability,

\[ \operatorname {Var}(R_i)=x_i\sum _{d\leq k{\lt}m}\sigma _k^2. \]
Proof ▶

The integral commutes with the finite sum. Pairwise independence supplied by BF1 makes the finite-sum variance additive.

Definition 27.3 Random BF estimate and MSEP
✓

For a random initial ultimate \(U\) and a random selected cumulative pattern \(z\), the BF estimate is \(\widehat R^{BF}=U(1-z)\). The unconditional MSEP is \(E[(\widehat R^{BF}-R)^2]\). Mack’s source conditions on the observed increments; common independence means that the pair consisting of estimate and future reserve is independent of the observed-data sigma-algebra.

If \(U\) and \(z\) are independent and the displayed products are square-integrable, then

\[ \operatorname {Var}\bigl(U(1-z)\bigr) =\bigl(E[U]^2+\operatorname {Var}(U)\bigr)\operatorname {Var}(z) +\operatorname {Var}(U)(1-E[z])^2. \]

For \(i{\lt}n\) under BF1, BF3, the stated square-integrability assumptions, and independence and equal means of the BF estimate and true future reserve,

\[ \operatorname {msep}(\widehat R^{BF}) =\operatorname {Var}(\widehat R^{BF}) +x_i\sum _{d\leq k{\lt}m}\sigma _k^2. \]

Under common independence from an observed-data sigma-algebra, square integrability, and measurability of the prediction-target pair, the conditional expectation of the squared prediction error equals that same constant almost surely.

Proof ▶

Factor first and second product moments by independence. For MSEP, center the prediction error and add the variances of the independent estimate and target.

Definition 27.5 Raw pattern and variance estimators

On a finite set \(A\) of accident years with known volumes \(x_i\), equations (1) and (2) become

\[ \widehat y_k=\frac{\sum _{i\in A}S_{ik}}{\sum _{i\in A}x_i},\qquad \widehat\sigma _k^2=\frac1{|A|-1}\sum _{i\in A} \frac{(S_{ik}-x_i\widehat y_k)^2}{x_i}. \]

A general linear estimator is \(\sum _{i\in A}a_iS_{ik}\).

For nonnegative volumes with nonzero total, the raw estimator is unbiased and has variance \(\sigma _k^2/\sum _i x_i\). If \(\sigma _k^2\geq 0\), every linear unbiased competitor, \(\sum _i a_i x_i=1\), has at least that variance. With at least two contributors and nonzero individual volumes, the residual estimator is unbiased for \(\sigma _k^2\).

Proof ▶

Independence makes the linear-estimator variance a diagonal quadratic form. Complete its square around \(a_i=1/\sum _jx_j\). For the residual estimator, subtract the fitted-mean square from the true-centre sum; their expectations are \(\sigma _k^2\) times \(|A|\) and one, respectively.

Equations (3), (4), (6), and (7) define the raw development proportions, raw column variances, squared standard errors of the selected proportions, and the minimum of the prefix and suffix variance sums. The single-year plug-in is

\[ \widehat{\operatorname {msep}} =\widehat{\operatorname {Var}}(R_i) +(U_i^2+s_U^2)s_z^2+s_U^2(1-z)^2. \]

The single-year assessment is exactly its named process term plus its two estimation terms. Before Mack omits the middle term for the total-reserve approximation, independence of the pairs \(\{ X,W\} \) and \(\{ Y,Z\} \) gives

\[ \operatorname {Cov}(XY,WZ) =\operatorname {Cov}(X,W)E[Y]E[Z] +\operatorname {Cov}(X,W)\operatorname {Cov}(Y,Z) +E[X]E[W]\operatorname {Cov}(Y,Z). \]
Proof ▶

On the one-point probability space, take \(C_{i,k}=k+1\), so every increment is one, \(x_i=y_k=1\), and \(\sigma _k^2=0\) on the one-period horizon. BF1–BF3 and the normalization hold together. The future reserve has mean one and variance zero, and prediction by one has conditional MSEP zero. This checks satisfiability of the hypotheses; it does not provide a positive-variance BF model.

Proof ▶

Every random variable is constant. The generated sigma-algebras are trivial, so the full increment family is independent.
