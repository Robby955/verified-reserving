23 Calendar-year claims development results

Merz and Wüthrich condition on the cells available after a calendar diagonal [ 7 ] . This chapter gives that filtration directly and keeps their Appendix A.1 linear approximation separate from the exact probability statements.

Definition 23.1 Calendar-year information
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.RandomTriangle.calendarSigma
  • VerifiedReserving.RandomTriangle.otherCalendar

At calendar time \(t\),

\[ \mathcal D^{\mathrm{cal}}_t = \sigma \{ C_{i,j}: i{\lt}n,\ j{\lt}n,\ i+j\le t\} . \]

The companion sigma-algebra otherCalendar contains the observed cells from every row except a specified accident year.

Theorem 23.2 The calendar triangle splits at one row
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 23.1
  • Definition 5.26
  • Theorem 5.28

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.RandomTriangle.calendarSigma_mono
  • VerifiedReserving.RandomTriangle.calendarSigma_eq_sup
  • VerifiedReserving.condExp_calendarSigma_eq_rowSigma

For \(i{\lt}n\) and \(k{\lt}n\), at time \(i+k\) the calendar triangle is row \(i\) through development \(k\) joined with the observed parts of the other rows. Under accident-year independence, conditioning a row-\(i\) random variable on this calendar triangle is the same as conditioning it on row \(i\) through \(k\).

Proof ▶

Split the generating joins at row \(i\), then apply conditioning on an independent enlargement.

\[ E[C_{i,n-1}\mid \mathcal D^{\mathrm{cal}}_t] -E[C_{i,n-1}\mid \mathcal D^{\mathrm{cal}}_{t+1}]. \]
Theorem 23.4 Exact calendar-year CDR
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 23.3
  • Theorem 23.2
  • Definition 5.2
  • Definition 5.13

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.condExp_calendarTrueCDR_eq_zero
  • VerifiedReserving.calendarTrueCDR_eq_of_rows
  • VerifiedReserving.condExp_sq_calendarTrueCDR_of_rows
  • VerifiedReserving.condVar_calendarTrueCDR_of_rows

The raw mean-zero equality needs only a finite measure; since conditional expectation is zero off the integrable case in the formalization, it has its intended probabilistic meaning when the ultimate is integrable. For \(i{\lt}n\) and \(k{\lt}n-1\), under the row-conditioned Mack assumptions, independent accident years, row integrability, and squared-residual integrability, at time \(i+k\) it equals

\[ \left(\prod _{j=k+1}^{n-2} f_j\right) (f_kC_{i,k}-C_{i,k+1}), \]

and its conditional second moment and conditional variance are \(\left(\prod _{j=k+1}^{n-2}f_j\right)^2\sigma _k^2C_{i,k}\).

Proof ▶

The tower property gives the mean-zero result. The split theorem reduces the other two statements to the row filtration and (M1row), (M3row).

Definition 23.5 Appendix A.1 boundary
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.mwA1ProductIncrement
  • VerifiedReserving.mwA1LinearApprox
  • VerifiedReserving.mwA1Remainder

For finite increments \(a_j\), the source replaces \(\prod _j(1+a_j)-1\) by the first-order expression \(\sum _j a_j\). Lean records the exact product, the linear approximation, and their difference as three separate definitions. No equality or statistical error bound is built into the approximation.

Theorem 23.6 Exact boundary identities
✓

The product increment is the linear approximation plus its exact remainder. For nonnegative increments the linear term is a lower bound, as stated after (A.1), and for two increments the discarded term is exactly \(ab\).

Proof ▶

Ring algebra and the finite Weierstrass product inequality.

Theorem 23.7 Finite CDR and MSEP witness
✓
#

Uses

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.IndependenceWitness.mack3Row

  • VerifiedReserving.IndependenceWitness.condMsep_witness

The eight-outcome independent-row model satisfies (M3row), instantiates the exact single-row and total MSEP wrappers, and has nonzero calendar CDR on three distinct updates: rows \(0\), \(1\), and \(2\) reveal their independent first-step shocks at calendar times \(0\), \(1\), and \(2\).

Proof ▶

Each row shock is Rademacher. The first residual has the prescribed second moment and every later residual vanishes.
