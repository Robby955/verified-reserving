21 The stochastic ODP layer

England and Verrall describe independent incremental claims with

\[ \mathbb {E}[X_{i,k}]=m_{i,k}, \qquad \operatorname {Var}(X_{i,k})=\varphi m_{i,k}, \qquad \log m_{i,k}=c+\alpha _i+\beta _k, \]

with \(\varphi {\gt}0\) and the corner constraints \(\alpha _0=\beta _0=0\) [ 4 , Section 2, equations (2.5)–(2.7), p. 283 ] . The same moment and link equations appear in [ 6 , Section 2, equations (2.1)–(2.3) ] . This chapter separates exact consequences of those assumptions from the first-order GLM prediction-error formula and the residual bootstrap algorithm.

21.1 Model and conditional moments

Definition 21.1 Finite stochastic ODP model

For a finite square of cells, the dispersion is positive and the mean surface admits a corner-constrained log-link representation. Every incremental claim is measurable and square-integrable, has that mean \(m_{i,k}\) and variance \(\varphi m_{i,k}\), and the family is mutually independent. In particular, every modeled mean is positive. The observed-data sigma-algebra is generated by any selected finite set of cells.

Lemma 21.2 Generated sigma-algebras and independence
✓

The sigma-algebras generated by one cell and by a finite vector of cells are sub-sigma-algebras of the ambient one. A cell outside the selected vector is independent of that vector.

Proof ▶

Measurability gives the two inclusions. Mutual independence, restricted to the disjoint singleton and observed index sets, gives the final statement.

Theorem 21.3 Exact conditional ODP moments
✓
#

Uses

Lean declarations

If \((i,k)\) is not among the observed cells, then

\[ \mathbb {E}[X_{i,k}\mid \mathcal{O}]=m_{i,k}, \qquad \mathbb {E}[(X_{i,k}-m_{i,k})^2\mid \mathcal{O}]=\varphi m_{i,k} \]

almost surely. These are exact conditional-moment identities, not GLM approximations.

Proof ▶

Independence makes each conditional expectation equal to its integral; substitute the two fields of the ODP model.

For a finite set \(F\) of future cells, let \(Y_F=\sum _{p\in F}X_p\). For a predictor \(\widehat Y\), define \(\operatorname {MSEP}(Y_F,\widehat Y) =\mathbb {E}[(Y_F-\widehat Y)^2]\).

Theorem 21.5 Aggregate mean and process variance
✓

Uses

For every finite \(F\),

\[ \mathbb {E}[Y_F]=\sum _{p\in F}m_p, \qquad \operatorname {Var}(Y_F)=\varphi \sum _{p\in F}m_p. \]
Proof ▶

Integrate the finite sum and add the variances of the independent square-integrable cells.

Theorem 21.6 Exact process-plus-estimation decomposition
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 21.4
  • Theorem 21.5

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.meanSquaredPredictionError_eq_variance_add
  • VerifiedReserving.odpAggregateMsep_eq

If a square-integrable predictor has the same mean as its square-integrable target and is independent of it, then

\[ \mathbb {E}[(Y-\widehat Y)^2]=\operatorname {Var}(Y)+\operatorname {Var}(\widehat Y). \]

For an ODP aggregate this is \(\varphi \sum _{p\in F}m_p+\operatorname {Var}(\widehat Y)\).

Proof ▶

The difference has mean zero. Expand its variance and use independence to set the covariance to zero, then substitute Theorem 21.5.

England and Verrall use the delta method for the estimation component: since \(\widehat m=\exp (\widehat\eta )\), \(\operatorname {Var}(\widehat m)\mathrel {\dot=}\widehat m^2 \operatorname {Var}(\widehat\eta )\). Their single-cell and aggregate formulas are equations (3.3)–(3.5) of [ 4 ] and equations (7.7)–(7.9) of [ 5 ] . The dotted equality is not promoted to a theorem.

Definition 21.7 Delta-method GLM MSEP formulas

For a finite aggregate, the estimation term is the covariance quadratic form \(\sum _{p,q}\widehat m_p\widehat m_q\operatorname {Cov}(\widehat\eta _p,\widehat\eta _q)\), added to \(\varphi \sum _p\widehat m_p\). 21.3 Log-link and corner constraints

Definition 21.9 Log-link and corner effects
✓

The log-link fit is \(\exp (c+\alpha _i+\beta _k)\). For positive multiplicative parameters \(a_i,b_k\), set \(\alpha _i=\log a_i-\log a_0\) and \(\beta _k=\log b_k-\log b_0\).

Theorem 21.10 Log-link and multiplicative equivalence
✓
#

Uses

  • VerifiedReserving.cornerRow_zero

21.4 Residual bootstrap definitions

England and Verrall’s unscaled Pearson residual, scale estimate, and inversion are equations (4.2)–(4.4) of [ 4 ] . England’s later two-stage procedure first resamples adjusted residuals and refits the mean, then simulates future process outcomes conditional on that mean [ 6 , Sections 2–3 ] .

Definition 21.11 Pearson residual operations
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.pearsonResidual
  • VerifiedReserving.pearsonScale
  • VerifiedReserving.adjustedPearsonResidual
  • VerifiedReserving.bootstrapIncremental

For an observation \(x\) and fitted mean \(m\), the unscaled Pearson residual is \(r=(x-m)/\sqrt m\). The Pearson scale is \(\sum r^2/(N-p)\), the degrees-of-freedom adjustment multiplies a residual by \(\sqrt{N/(N-p)}\), and inversion gives the pseudo-observation \(x^*=r^*\sqrt m+m\). Lean’s total division leaves these definitions meaningful at zero; the inversion theorems below assume \(m{\gt}0\).

Lemma 21.12 Pearson inversion
✓

Uses

For \(m{\gt}0\), taking the residual of \(r\sqrt m+m\) returns \(r\), and rebuilding an observation from its Pearson residual returns that observation.

Cancel the nonzero factor \(\sqrt m\).

Definition 21.13 England-Verrall bootstrap and predictive reserve
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.englandVerrallBootstrapMean
  • VerifiedReserving.englandVerrallPredictiveReserve

One bootstrap refit resamples supplied residuals with replacement, inverts them against the fitted past means, and applies a supplied refitting map to obtain future means. The predictive reserve sums supplied process draws conditional on those bootstrap means. The process simulator is an input: the ODP moment equations do not determine a unique distribution. These declarations define the algorithm and make no coverage or consistency claim.

On the product of four uniform two-point spaces, one coordinate is assigned to each cell of a \(2\) by \(2\) square. Every claim is \(0\) or \(2\), has mean and variance one, and the four claims are mutually independent. The unit mean surface satisfies the corner-constrained log link with zero effects and \(\varphi =1\). Taking \((0,1)\) as future and the nonempty singleton \(\{ (0,0)\} \) as observed instantiates both conditional-moment theorems. Two random coordinates also instantiate the exact MSEP decomposition with value \(2\).

Proof ▶

The coordinate projections are independent under the finite product measure. Transport the two-point mean and variance along each measure-preserving evaluation map.
