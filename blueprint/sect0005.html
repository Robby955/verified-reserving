5 The stochastic layer

5.1 Model

Definition 5.1 Random triangle
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.RandomTriangle

A random run-off triangle on a measurable space \(\Omega \) is a family of random variables \(C_{i,k} : \Omega \to \mathbb {R}\) together with a filtration \(\mathcal{D}_k\) such that \(C_{i,j}\) is \(\mathcal{D}_k\)-measurable whenever \(j \le k\) ("everything observed up to development year \(k\)").

Definition 5.2 Assumption (M1)
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 5.1

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.Mack1

\(E[C_{i,k+1} \mid \mathcal{D}_k] = f_k\, C_{i,k}\) almost surely, for all \(i\) and \(k\). This is Mack’s first assumption in the form conditioned on \(\mathcal{D}_k\); Mack states it conditioned on the row’s own history and obtains this form from independence across accident years (M2), a passage still to be formalized.

5.2 Mack’s Theorem 2

Theorem 5.5 Conditional unbiasedness of the development factor
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 5.2
  • Definition 5.3
  • Lemma 5.4

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.condExp_fhatRv

Assume (M1), that \(S_k \neq 0\) almost surely, that each \(C_{i,k+1}\) and \(\hat f_k\) are integrable. Then \(E[\hat f_k \mid \mathcal{D}_k] = f_k\) almost surely.

Proof ▶

\(\hat f_k = S_k^{-1} \sum _i C_{i,k+1}\). The factor \(S_k^{-1}\) is \(\mathcal{D}_k\)-measurable and comes out of the conditional expectation; by (M1) the conditional expectation of the sum is \(f_k \sum _i C_{i,k} = f_k S_k\); cancel \(S_k\) where it is nonzero.

Theorem 5.7 Uncorrelated development factors, conditional form
✓
#

Uses

  • Theorem 5.5
  • Lemma 5.6

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.condExp_fhatRv_mul

Under (M1), on a finite measure space, for \(j {\lt} k\) (with the integrability and \(S \neq 0\) hypotheses of Theorem 5.5 for both columns and \(\hat f_j \hat f_k\) integrable), \(E[\hat f_j \hat f_k \mid \mathcal{D}_j] = f_j f_k\) almost surely.

Proof ▶

Condition on \(\mathcal{D}_k\) first: \(\hat f_j\) is \(\mathcal{D}_k\)-measurable and comes out, and \(E[\hat f_k \mid \mathcal{D}_k] = f_k\) by Theorem 5.5. Then the tower property down to \(\mathcal{D}_j\) and Theorem 5.5 again for column \(j\).

Theorem 5.8 Mack 1993, Theorem 2
✓

Lemma 5.11 Iterating (M1)
✓
#

For \(d \le k\), \(E[C_{i,k+1} \mid \mathcal{D}_d] = f_k\, E[C_{i,k} \mid \mathcal{D}_d]\), hence \(E[C_{i,d+m} \mid \mathcal{D}_d] = C_{i,d}\prod _{k=d}^{d+m-1} f_k\).

Theorem 5.12 Mack 1993, Theorem 1
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 5.10
  • Theorem 5.5
  • Lemma 5.11

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.condExp_ChatRv
  • VerifiedReserving.condExp_ultimate_eq

Under (M1), with the partial products integrable and the column sums a.s. nonzero, \(E[\hat C_{i,d+m} \mid \mathcal{D}_d] = C_{i,d}\prod _{k=d}^{d+m-1} f_k\), and therefore \(E[\hat C_{i,n-1} \mid \mathcal{D}_d] = E[C_{i,n-1} \mid \mathcal{D}_d]\): the chain-ladder ultimate is a conditionally unbiased estimator of the true ultimate claims.

Proof ▶

Condition on \(\mathcal{D}_{d+m}\): the partial product comes out and the last factor has conditional expectation \(f_{d+m}\) (Theorem 5.5); tower down to \(\mathcal{D}_d\) and induct. Compare with Lemma 5.11.

5.4 The variance assumptions and the estimation variance of \(\hat f_k\)

Definition 5.13 Residuals, (M3) and (M2’)
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 5.1

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.RandomTriangle.eps
  • VerifiedReserving.Mack3
  • VerifiedReserving.Mack2'

With \(\varepsilon _{i,k} = C_{i,k+1} - f_k C_{i,k}\): (M3) \(E[\varepsilon _{i,k}^2 \mid \mathcal{D}_k] = \sigma _k^2 C_{i,k}\), and (M2’) \(E[\varepsilon _{i,k}\varepsilon _{j,k} \mid \mathcal{D}_k] = 0\) for \(i \neq j\), the conditional uncorrelatedness across accident years that independence of accident years supplies.

Lemma 5.14
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 5.3
  • Definition 5.13

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.RandomTriangle.fhatRv_sub_eq
  • VerifiedReserving.RandomTriangle.sq_fhatRv_sub

On \(\{ S_k \neq 0\} \), \(\hat f_k - f_k = S_k^{-1}\sum _i \varepsilon _{i,k}\) and \((\hat f_k - f_k)^2 = S_k^{-2}\sum _i\sum _j \varepsilon _{i,k}\varepsilon _{j,k}\).

Proof ▶

\(T_k - f_k S_k = \sum _i \varepsilon _{i,k}\); expand the square of the sum.

Theorem 5.15 Estimation variance of the development factor
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 5.14
  • Lemma 5.4

Lean declarations

  • VerifiedReserving.condExp_sq_fhatRv_sub

Under (M3) and (M2’), with the residual products integrable, on \(\{ S_k \neq 0\} \), \(E[(\hat f_k - f_k)^2 \mid \mathcal{D}_k] = \sigma _k^2 / S_k\) (Mack 1993, proof of Theorem 3; Mack 1999, \(\mathrm{s.e.}(\hat f_k)^2 = \hat\sigma _k^2/S_k\)).

Proof ▶

\(S_k^{-2}\) is \(\mathcal{D}_k\)-measurable and comes out; conditional expectation is linear over the double sum; cross terms vanish by (M2’); diagonal terms give \(\sigma _k^2\sum _i C_{i,k} = \sigma _k^2 S_k\).

5.5 Open: variance estimator and the MSEP theorem

Theorem 5.16 Unbiasedness of the variance estimator
#

Uses

  • Definition 5.13
  • Theorem 5.8
  • Theorem 5.15
  • Lemma 2.12

Under (M1) and (M3), \(E[\hat\sigma _k^2 \mid \mathcal{D}_k] = \sigma _k^2\) for \(k \le n-3\).

Proof ▶

Lemma 2.12 reduces the sum of squares to \(\sum _i C_{i,k}F_{i,k}^2 - S_k\hat f_k^2\); take conditional expectations using (M3) and the conditional variance of \(\hat f_k\), which is \(\sigma _k^2 / S_k\).

Theorem 5.17 Mack’s Theorem 3
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.8
  • Theorem 5.8
  • Theorem 5.16

Under (M1)-(M3), Mack’s estimator \(\widehat{\mathrm{msep}}(\hat R_i)\) is the sum of the process variance \(\hat C_{i,n-1}^2\sum _k \hat\sigma _k^2/(\hat f_k^2 \hat C_{i,k})\) and the estimation-error term \(\hat C_{i,n-1}^2\sum _k \hat\sigma _k^2/(\hat f_k^2 S_k)\), where the estimation-error term is obtained by Mack’s conditional-resampling step (replacing \((\hat f_k - f_k)^2\) by \(\sigma _k^2/S_k\) and dropping cross terms by Theorem 5.7). The statement to formalize is the estimator as Mack defines it; the exact conditional MSEP is a different object (Buchwalder, Bühlmann, Merz and Wüthrich 2006).

Proof ▶

Iterated conditional variance along the row for the process part; Mack’s approximation step for the estimation part.
